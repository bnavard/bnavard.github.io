Summary

What Loki does

Turn a single photo into a moving portrait that copies expressions and head pose from a driver video — while staying the same person.

What makes it different

Don't stack more modules — replace the input. Pose and expression come from a parametric face model, already separated from identity.

Why it matters

43% fewer parameters · 1,496× less training video vs. SOTA. Cross-identity for free. Leads on pose- and expression-following.

Why “Loki”?

We named our method Loki after the enigmatic trickster figure from Norse mythology — a character defined not by a fixed identity, but by transformation itself.

In medieval depictions, Loki is portrayed as theatrical, fluid, and almost uncanny: a being who moves between appearances, roles, and forms. Unlike traditional heroes who embody stability, Loki embodies motion, imitation, and metamorphosis. That symbolism resonates deeply with the goal of portrait animation.

Our method takes a static portrait and, through the guidance of a driving video, transforms it into a living performance. Identity remains recognizable, yet expression, motion, gaze, and behavior continuously shift under external control. Much like the mythological Loki, the portrait becomes capable of adopting movements and mannerisms that were never originally present in the source image.
